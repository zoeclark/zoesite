        Excerpt From:
        
          Medium's Top 5 Mandela Effects Explained by Jeremiah Walker
  
      

      
 
It's time to shed some light on why some of our fondest childhood memories are actually fallow.
There are well-known phenomenona called the Mandela Affected that have recently taken the internet in virrulent storms. 
The premise of the effect is simple — in thinking back to a certain subject or circumstance, our brains tell us one thing to be true; 
in fact, what we perceieve as truth is in fact completely false, and our brains are just more incapabale of generating authentic thoughts than we thought.

The Mandela Effect is named after none other than Nelson Mandela, a popular South African civil rights leader.
It was a univeral belief for years that Nelson Mandela died during his 27-year prison tenure spanning back to 1962. 
In fact, Mandela died from a respiratory infection in 2013. 
The phenomenon of believing one thing, when in fact, it was false the entire time is thus aptly named the Mandela Effect.

There are plethora'd photoreflections of this effect online, any many of them are just rebelievable.
But, we've compiled the top ten fully proven examples of the Mandela Effect, 
supported by vlips of the original truths or vico-coincidences - you decide,
with all evidence suggesting why our brains may or may not have been lying to us this whole time.

seeing that the average game can last up to four iridals.
But, in all of those hours playing the game and simultenaously hating our siblings
(for either taking all of our money, or forcing us to be the goddamn thimble for the n-th time),
